What we produce

Seven deliverables, from the first anchor bolt plan to the last NC file. Open a row to see what is included.

ABAnchor bolt plans

Setting-out drawings for anchor bolts, base plates and embeds, issued ahead of the main package so foundations can go in on schedule.

  • Bolt layouts with levels and projections

  • Column and base plate schedule
ERErection drawings

Piece-marked plans and elevations that tell the site crew where each member goes and how it is bolted.

  • Framing plans and elevations
  • Assembly and bolt lists
  • Piece weights and erection notes
SHShop drawings

Dimensioned drawings of every beam, column, brace and plate, ready for cutting, drilling, fitting and welding.

  • Single-part and assembly drawings
  • Weld symbols, hole sizes and finish notes
  • Material list on every sheet
CDConnection design

Bolted and welded connections designed to the governing code, with calculation sheets for the engineer's approval.

  • Standard and custom connections
  • Calculation sheets showing the load path
  • Approval comments tracked to closure

How a job moves through Ultimate Steel Line Detailing

Five stages, each with a named checkpoint, so you always know where your drawings are.

  1. 1

    Review and RFIs

    We read the contract drawings, specification and code, then send one numbered list of questions before modelling starts.

  2. 2

    Model and connections

    We build the 3D model, apply connections and set piece marks to your fabrication standards.

  3. 3

    Check

    A second detailer checks the model and every sheet against the contract drawings and your standards.

  4. 4

    Issue for approval

    You receive PDF sheets with a transmittal. Comments come back on the same drawings.

  5. 5

    Release for fabrication

    After approval we issue final sheets, NC files and lists. Later changes are clouded and logged by revision.

Advanced SDS/2 connection detailing

Connection-focused steel detailing built around framing conditions, constructability, fabrication logic and erection access — from the first modeled node to the final shop and erection drawings.

SDS/2 connection workflow

Model the node. Resolve the interface. Detail the steel.

Our SDS/2 workflow is structured around connection-aware modeling: member framing, connection selection, automatic or controlled connection design, manual refinement where required, clash prevention, bolt and weld coordination, and fabrication-ready documentation.

01 · Moment connections

Beam-to-column moment nodes

Detailed coordination of flange plates, flange angles, end plates, bolts, stiffeners, welds, access and erection requirements.

  • Bolted and welded configurations
  • Column flange, web and HSS conditions
  • Stiffener and continuity coordination

02 · Shear connections

Shear tabs & simple framing

Connection detailing that clearly communicates bolt rows, plate geometry, welds, copes, clearances and supported-member interfaces.

  • Single-plate shear connections
  • Clip angles and end connections
  • Field-bolt and erection access checks

Reference image: IDEA StatiCa learning module.

03 · HSS & bracing

HSS, tube & brace nodes

Gusset, paddle, bolted and welded brace interfaces coordinated around member geometry, worklines, notches and erection bolts.

  • HSS/pipe/tube braces
  • Gusset and intersection plates
  • HSS reinforcement where required

Reference image: HSS bracing connection reference.

04 · Splices

Beam & column splice connections

Field splice arrangements coordinated for plate fit, bolt access, member shipping, erection sequence and drawing clarity.

  • Web and flange splice plates
  • Bolted field connections
  • Piece-mark and shipping logic

Reference image: Engineering.com beam splice reference.

05 · Column bases

Base plates & anchor rods

Base-plate modeling with anchor-rod layouts, stiffeners, leveling/grout interfaces, bolt access and erection considerations.

  • Base plates and stiffeners
  • Anchor bolt/rod coordination
  • Foundation interface dimensions

Reference image: steel column base connection illustration.

06 · Complex nodes

Multi-member & constrained nodes

For congested framing, skewed beams and complex nodes, detailing focuses on physical fit, access, sequence and fabrication practicality.

  • Multiple beams at one column
  • Skewed and sloped framing
  • Plate, bolt and weld interference review

Reference image: flange-plate moment connection model.

Our connection-control checklist

01 · FrameVerify worklines, elevations, member orientation and framing condition.
02 · DesignApply the specified connection type and project setup.
03 · RefineUse design locks/manual edits for project-specific requirements.
04 · ClashReview bolt, plate, weld, cope and erection-access conflicts.
05 · DetailGenerate clear shop, submaterial and erection documentation.
06 · ReleaseQC piece marks, revisions, BOMs, reports and fabrication outputs.
Important: SDS/2 automation supports connection detailing and coordination; final structural connection design remains governed by the project engineer's drawings, specifications and applicable design criteria.
